Every serious JEE aspirant has heard the phrase "solve PYQs." But most guides stop there — they tell you *to* do PYQs without telling you *where* to get the best ones, *which* books are actually worth buying, or *how* to make the most of them.

This guide covers all three. You will leave knowing exactly where every reliable PYQ source lives, which compilations are worth your money, and a practical system to extract maximum value from each question you touch.

## Part 1: The Official Source — Always Start Here

Before diving into third-party books or websites, understand that the most authoritative archive for JEE papers lives on two official government domains.

### JEE Main Official Archive

The National Testing Agency (NTA) maintains the [official JEE Main portal](https://jeemain.nta.nic.in). Under its **"Question Papers"** or **"Archive"** section, you can find:

- Official question papers for Paper 1 (B.E./B.Tech), Paper 2A (B.Arch), and Paper 2B (B.Planning)
- Multiple session papers (January and April sessions since 2019, and shift-wise papers)
- Provisional and final answer keys
- Recorded candidate responses

**Why this matters:** Third-party sources sometimes have transcription errors or modified options. The official PDF is the canonical, error-free version. Always verify a suspicious question against the official portal.

👉 You can access these official papers directly from [JEE Main Official Papers](/official-links/jee-main) on JEE Challenger — curated and organized for quick access.

### JEE Advanced Official Archive

The [official JEE Advanced archive](https://jeeadv.ac.in) is one of the most valuable free resources available. It hosts:

- Complete question papers from **2007 onwards** (Paper 1 and Paper 2)
- Both English and Hindi versions
- Official answer keys

The archive goes back nearly two decades. This is a gold mine, especially for Advanced aspirants — the question style has evolved significantly over the years, and studying this evolution is itself an insight into how IITs think.

👉 JEE Challenger also curates [JEE Advanced Official Papers](/official-links/jee-advanced) for convenient access.

## Part 2: Best Free Websites for Chapter-wise PYQs

Official archives give you full papers. But when you are still building chapter by chapter, you need questions sorted by topic. These platforms do exactly that, for free.

### 1. ExamSIDE — Top Pick for Organization

[ExamSIDE](https://www.examside.com) is widely regarded as the cleanest and best-organized chapter-wise PYQ platform available. Key features:

- **Filter by subject, chapter, and year** with a single interface
- Questions from both JEE Main (2002–present) and JEE Advanced (2007–present)
- Clean solution display with minimal clutter
- No login required for most content

**Best used for:** Rapid chapter-level drilling immediately after finishing theory. The filtering system is unmatched.

### 2. MathonGo — Best Analytics for JEE Main

[MathonGo](https://www.mathongo.com) started as a Mathematics platform and has expanded to cover all three subjects. It is particularly strong for:

- Chapter-wise JEE Main PYQs with quality solutions
- Weightage analysis — it shows you how frequently each topic appears
- A clean UI designed specifically for exam practice

**Best used for:** Understanding which chapters are highest priority in JEE Main, based on hard data from actual paper frequency.

### 3. SATHEE by IIT Kanpur — Government-Backed and Completely Free

[SATHEE](https://sathee.iitk.ac.in) (Self-Assessment Test and Help for Entrance Exams) is a free initiative by IIT Kanpur, endorsed by the Ministry of Education. It provides:

- Free chapter-wise PYQs with solutions
- Video solutions for selected questions
- Full coverage of the JEE syllabus

**Why it stands out:** Government-backed, IIT-curated content. The explanations are rigorous and trustworthy, making it one of the most reliable free platforms available.

### 4. Allen — Best for JEE Advanced PYQs

[Allen](https://www.allen.ac.in) provides excellent chapter-wise question banks specifically curated for JEE Advanced preparation. The difficulty level and solution depth match the Advanced standard well.

**Best used for:** Advanced-level problem practice chapter by chapter, particularly in Physics where Advanced questions need a different problem-solving mindset compared to Main.

### 5. EduRev — Best for Recent Years (2018–2026)

[EduRev](https://www.edurev.in) has a comprehensive collection of chapter-wise PYQs spanning recent years. The platform is useful if you want to focus specifically on understanding how the exam has evolved in the last 5–8 years under the NTA format.

### 6. Vedantu and eSaral

[Vedantu](https://www.vedantu.com) and [eSaral](https://www.esaral.com) both offer free downloadable PDF compilations organized by subject and year. These are particularly useful for offline study or for students who prefer a printed, annotatable format without spending money on physical books.

## Part 3: Best Books for PYQ Practice

Websites are excellent for digital drilling. But for deep, focused preparation — especially full-paper simulation — a well-organized PYQ book is irreplaceable. Here is a comparison of the most popular options.

### For JEE Advanced

#### 1. 41 Years Chapterwise Topicwise Solved Papers — Arihant

**Coverage:** JEE and IIT-JEE papers from 1978 to recent years (updated annually)

**Strengths:**
- Longest history of coverage — 40+ years of questions gives you enormous pattern depth
- Organized by chapter *and* topic within each chapter (not just year-wise)
- Includes concept-anchor markers to highlight key ideas
- Separate volumes for Physics, Chemistry, and Mathematics

**Weakness:** Some older solutions use dated methods. Cross-check with modern approaches where applicable.

**Verdict:** The gold standard for JEE Advanced PYQ practice. Most top coaching institutes use this as their primary reference.

#### 2. 37 Years JEE (Advanced) Solved Papers — MTG

**Coverage:** 1979–present (updated annually)

**Strengths:**
- Extremely comprehensive question bank with alternate solution approaches
- Reliable solution accuracy
- Includes JEE Advanced pattern analysis sections

**Verdict:** Strong alternative to Arihant, particularly if you want the maximum number of questions in a single volume.

### For JEE Main

#### 3. JEE Main Chapterwise Solutions — Disha Publication

**Coverage:** JEE Main papers from 2002 onwards

**Strengths:**
- Clear separation of JEE Main and JEE Advanced questions
- Chapter-wise segregation with difficulty labeling
- User-friendly layout, well-suited for self-study
- Frequently updated to include the latest sessions

**Weakness:** Occasionally skips repetitive questions from early years — verify if you need pre-2010 depth.

**Verdict:** Best book for JEE Main chapter-wise PYQ practice.

#### 4. 15 Years' Solved Papers JEE Main — MTG

**Coverage:** JEE Main 2009–present

**Strengths:**
- Reliable for recent-year question coverage
- Good solution detail with alternate methods
- Available as a single-volume combined subject book, convenient for quick review

### Quick Comparison Table

| Book | Best For | Coverage | Organization |
|---|---|---|---|
| Arihant 41 Years | JEE Advanced deep prep | 1978–present | Chapter + Topic wise |
| MTG 37 Years | JEE Advanced max questions | 1979–present | Chapter wise |
| Disha Chapter-wise | JEE Main primary PYQ book | 2002–present | Chapter wise |
| MTG 15 Years | JEE Main quick coverage | 2009–present | Year wise |

**Practical recommendation:** Do not buy all four. For most aspirants, **Arihant 41 Years + Disha JEE Main** is the optimal combination. Add MTG only if you want additional question volume in a specific subject.

## Part 6: How Many Years of PYQs Do You Actually Need?

This is a common source of anxiety. Here is a practical benchmark based on what produces measurable results.

### JEE Main

- **Minimum effective coverage:** 10 years (roughly 2015–2025) — captures modern NTA patterns post-format change
- **Optimal coverage:** 15–20 years — adds pattern depth, especially for Physics and Mathematics
- **Beyond 20 years:** Diminishing returns; invest that time in mock tests instead

> **Critical note for JEE Main:** Since the NTA took over in 2019, the exam shifted to a computer-based format with multiple sessions and a higher proportion of numerical answer-type (NAT) questions. The 2019–present papers are the most directly relevant to what you will face. Prioritize them.

### JEE Advanced

- **Minimum effective coverage:** 10 years — captures IIT's thinking patterns post-2012 format shift (when Advanced became its own separate exam)
- **Optimal coverage:** 15 years — covers the most analytically rich era of Advanced papers
- **Beyond 15 years:** Very useful for concept depth (pre-2012 IIT-JEE papers are excellent training), but not necessary for most aspirants

**The quality hierarchy always matters more than year count:**
1. Attempt under timed conditions
2. Analyze every wrong and guessed answer
3. Extract the underlying pattern
4. Reattempt after spaced intervals
5. Log your correction rule

If steps 2–5 are missing from your process, adding more years of PYQs will not help.

## Part 7: A Practical 3-Phase PYQ Workflow

Having access to great resources only matters if you use them systematically. Here is a workflow that integrates PYQs into your daily routine without overwhelming you.

### Phase 1 — During Syllabus Coverage (Chapter-by-Chapter)

After completing theory + basic practice for any chapter:

1. Open ExamSIDE, MathonGo, or JEE Challenger's [Chapter-wise Solved PYQs](/previous-year-questions) and filter for that chapter
2. Solve 15–20 JEE Main PYQs from the chapter under timed conditions (allow roughly 2 minutes per question)
3. For JEE Advanced aspirants: add 5–10 JEE Advanced PYQs from the same chapter
4. Review every wrong and guessed question with its solution
5. Note the concept and question pattern in a dedicated error log notebook

**Rule of thumb:** Never move past a chapter without doing at least 10 PYQs from it.

### Phase 2 — During Revision (Full Papers)

Once 80% or more of your syllabus is complete:

1. Pick one full JEE Main paper per week from the official archive or your Arihant/Disha book
2. Simulate real exam conditions — 3 hours, no interruptions, no extra time
3. Score and analyze: which sections cost you time? Where were the pattern mistakes?
4. Maintain a separate notebook for full-paper patterns distinct from your chapter error logs

### Phase 3 — Final 3 Weeks Before the Exam

1. Revisit your error logs and correction-rule notes — *not* new questions
2. Complete 2–3 full papers per week for stamina and pacing
3. Focus exclusively on high-frequency chapters identified in Part 5 for last-minute solidification
